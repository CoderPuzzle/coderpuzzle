# Solutions — Minimum Cost to Make Array Equalindromic

A special move just sets one element to any value and charges the absolute
difference, so making the array equalindromic means choosing one
palindromic target y and paying sum |nums[i] - y| — the classic cost of
approximating a multiset by a single point, with y restricted to the
palindromes below 10⁹.

## Move the median to the nearest palindrome

sum |nums[i] - y| is a convex, piecewise-linear function of y: it
decreases while y is below the median and increases once y passes it (for
even n it is exactly flat between the two middle elements). The best
palindrome therefore brackets the median — the largest palindrome at or
below it, or the smallest at or above it — and no other palindrome can
win: walking further from the median only increases the cost. Both
candidates are evaluated and the cheaper one is returned; when the median
itself is palindromic the two candidates coincide.

Each candidate cost is a linear scan. The palindromes below 10⁹ number
109,998 and are generated by mirroring every half 1..99999 into its odd-
and even-length palindrome; the bracketing pair of the median is then
located by binary search over the sorted table. Sorting dominates: O(n
log n) time against a fixed-size table, O(n) space. Totals reach
n × max|nums[i] − y| ≤ 10⁵ × 10⁹ = 10¹⁴, which requires 64-bit
accumulators in the typed languages and stays below 2⁵³, so JavaScript
numbers remain exact.

**Complexity:** `O(n log n)` time, `O(n)` space.
